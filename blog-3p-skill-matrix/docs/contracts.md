# Shared contracts

## Canonical record

Every article has one `article_id` and one canonical source. The package manifest records `canonical_sha256`; a reader-visible change to the canonical source or visual payload creates a new fingerprint and re-enters W → R → G. A public-page observation alone follows the public reader-page contract below and does not reopen W/R. `context/article-contract.json` and `reviews/review-index.json` are compact, hash-bound navigation records; neither can alter canonical content, owner scope or an approved verdict.

## Role contract

| Role | May do | Must not do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| W | Draft and repair canonical content | Review its own work or decide release |
| R | Independently inspect and issue findings | Edit the article or change scope |
| G | Collect and report the pre-write plan, freeze the owner contract after confirmation, dispatch, decide contract acceptance and update state | Draft text, claim W research is complete, re-review article quality, silently resolve findings or publish |
| Human | Use a native platform UI, accept or flag the reader page, and save the URL/state return receipt | Change canonical content without a correction record |

## Orchestration contract

### Pre-write owner-confirmation contract

Before any article worktree, article lane G, W, R, article queue, outline, prose, image, canonical package, or article-research subagent exists, campaign G maintains canonical `prewrite-plan.json` and runs `harnessctl.py sync-prewrite-plan` to render the owner-facing `prewrite-plan.md`. In current schema-2.4 workspaces the manifest is schema `1.3`; the Markdown report is a deterministic read-only view, never a separately hand-maintained plan. The dossier has one card for every configured `article_id`, covering task/audience, independent reader value and the required secondary CTA's exact visible anchor text, destination, claim basis, relevance and disclosure, evidence and uncertainty, keyword/localization strategy, factual claim/source plan, provisional title/outline, visual narrative, platform transport assumptions, and risks/owner decisions. It also reports campaign task/scope, content-value/CTA policy, sources/uncertainty, shared constraints, and platform-matching status.

The owner must explicitly return `OWNER_PREWRITE_PLAN_CONFIRMED`. G then binds the exact article IDs, owner confirmation ID, report SHA-256, and manifest SHA-256 in state, confirmation, requirements contract, and scope lock before it may dispatch a lane. A material change invalidates the binding and requires a refreshed confirmation. `OWNER_PREWRITE_PLAN_CHANGES_REQUESTED` never permits a partial dispatch. The dossier is a plan and known-evidence record, not `RESEARCH_READY` or `RESEARCH_APPROVED`: after confirmation W still conducts integrated research and same-article R still approves it before prose.

### Article contract, review index, and delta capsule

At dispatch, the lane G writes one immutable `context/article-contract.json` for its article. It projects only the confirmed article-relevant scope: article ID, task/language/market, applicable `REQ-*`, reader-value/required CTA declaration, release-pair and locale references when applicable, plus paths and hashes for its confirmed campaign records. It is derived from the global scope lock and cannot add, remove or reinterpret a requirement.

R maintains `reviews/review-index.json` after every research review, full review and R-Δ. It indexes the latest decisions, current canonical/package hashes, report paths, open/resolved/superseded stable finding IDs and the next required input. Ordinary W/R/G turns load the contract, index and current changed artifacts; they do not reread every historic report merely because another bounded turn begins. A full historical reread is mandatory only after context compaction, any referenced hash drift, unresolved or ambiguous finding lineage, or explicit escalation; agent replacement and scope conflict are escalation conditions. The compact records point to evidence; they never overrule it.

An R-Δ begins with `reviews/review-delta-N.json`. It names the approved baseline hashes/report, exact changed paths, affected claims, `REQ-*` and stable finding IDs, and flags any source, keyword/localization, title/metadata, CTA/disclosure, image or visual-payload change. Missing or contradictory data escalates to full R. A valid capsule permits a bounded R-Δ over the changed artifacts and direct dependencies, while full R remains mandatory whenever the risk surface is not bounded.

### Current artifact-source contract

For a schema-2.4 workspace, `article-package.json` uses schema `1.3`. It points to the canonical `research/evidence-pack.json`, `canonical/visual-manifest.json`, and derived `handoff/handoff-manifest.json`; do not retell those records manually in parallel fields. W stabilizes text, SEO fields, links, sources and CTA first, then records final images in the visual manifest and asks R for the narrow visual-payload delta. Any non-visual semantic change follows the normal W → R path. R judges payload semantics; G checks only the manifest/hash chain and `REQ-*` traceability. Before hand-off, `final_visual_payload_delta` must be `APPROVED` and bind its report hash, the registered R ID, review-index hash, visual-manifest hash and visual-payload hash. The hand-off checker also requires completed visible task receipts from that same R for `RESEARCH_REVIEW`, `FULL_REVIEW`, and `VISUAL_PAYLOAD_DELTA`; this avoids a semantic G redo. Schema-2.2/2.3 workspaces retain their historical schema-`1.2` article-package contract unless deliberately migrated.

## Article-platform assignment contract

Before editorial dispatch for a human-release batch, freeze `article_platform_assignment.assignments[]`. It is a one-to-one mapping from every `article_id` to one owner-supplied `{platform, account}` in `platform_scope.allowed_pairs`. Article IDs, platform names and pairs must each be unique; a verified Blogger account, for example, is not a generic allocation pool. Missing/duplicate assignment is `ARTICLE_PLATFORM_ASSIGNMENT_BLOCKED_RECONFIRM_OWNER`. G may continue other fully mapped articles, but must not borrow, rotate, substitute or discover a platform; a remap needs fresh owner confirmation.

## Locale-platform compatibility contract

Freeze one `locale_platform_validation.rows[]` record per release article before W/R. A record contains its article ID, user-confirmed article language and market, mapped platform/account, the platform's supported content languages, evidence and `decision: COMPATIBLE`. The article language directs research and writing; platform languages decide eligibility only. If the target platform does not support the frozen article language, report `LOCALE_PLATFORM_MISMATCH_RECONFIRM_OWNER`; do not translate the article or automatically reassign its platform.

## Title quality and field-mapping contract

Freeze focus keyword/approved variants, reader task, canonical article title, explicit platform title and SEO title per article. W/R judge semantic quality from topic clarity, reader intent, distinct value, natural language and market fit; G only verifies that an owner-specified title or SEO task survives into delivery. Literal keyword coverage and language-appropriate length are advisory signals, never standalone failure tests. The default mapping is `platform_title = canonical_title`; a platform title field must never receive a shorter substitute while a stronger title exists elsewhere. A clearly keyword-only, generic, misleading or task-mismatched title is `SEO-TITLE-INTENT-NNN`; an unapproved mapping divergence is `TITLE-FIELD-MAP-NNN`. Repair all linked title fields, fingerprint and release card together, then re-review.

## Content-first recommendation contract

The primary outcome of every article is an evidence-backed, standalone answer to its frozen reader task. A campaign CTA is required in every new schema-2.2 article, but never a completion quota or substitute for instruction, analysis, examples, evidence, or a balanced product comparison. It remains a secondary, contextually relevant recommendation. An explicitly requested product review or comparison may have a product-specific reader task, but it must still answer that task independently rather than becoming sales copy.

For the required secondary CTA, freeze a non-empty `reader_value_promise` and `cta` declaration in the article record: exact visible `anchor_text`, product identity, current destination, product/claim evidence path, reader-task relevance, and exact relationship disclosure or `NOT_APPLICABLE`. A current schema-2.4 `article-package.json` uses schema `1.3`, references its canonical artifact sources, and copies those declarations exactly; `harnessctl.py check-article-package` verifies this mapping and the artifact chain without judging the prose, and the hand-off compiler/validator verify the visible anchor, href and applicable disclosure in the body. Existing schema-2.2/2.3 packages remain schema `1.2` under their historical contract. W must trace product claims to the source ledger and must not invent reliability, rankings, tests, availability, outcomes, or independent endorsement. R decides whether the article remains useful without the CTA and whether the recommendation is genuinely secondary, relevant, evidence-bounded, and transparent. G only checks that the owner-confirmed declaration and required CTA/disclosure survive into the canonical package and visual hand-off; it does not judge product quality or rewrite commercial language.

This policy reduces obvious promotional-spam and disclosure risk; it does not promise that a platform will retain a public page or replace current platform policies.

The owner pre-authorizes visible child-task delegation by default **after the pre-write confirmation passes**. Visibility, reusable role identity and Git worktree isolation are separate properties: a child task is not automatically a worktree. G records host capability before dispatch. When the project can create worktrees, campaign G creates one visible project-worktree task per ready independent article before the first W turn, waits for a usable worktree/task identifier, and records `GIT_WORKTREE`, task ID, path when exposed, base ref and lifecycle in `state.json.orchestration.article_workspaces[article_id]`. The worktree root must be a persistent `ARTICLE_LANE_GATEKEEPER`, never a W-only task. It creates that worktree's W and R, then drives `G → W ↔ R → G`. Its `role_bundle` records the lane G, W and R IDs. Campaign G retains the unique global scope, queue and ledger; the lane G can decide only whether that article's frozen requirements survived its delivery. When worktrees are unavailable, campaign G records `WORKTREE_UNAVAILABLE` with the reason; a visible shared-workspace fallback is permitted only when article paths are disjoint, must be recorded as `VISIBLE_SHARED_WORKSPACE_WITH_PATH_ISOLATION`, and must never be represented as a worktree. Overlapping paths are `WORKTREE_ISOLATION_BLOCKED` and remain queued.

Campaign G is the visible controller and persistent **global owner-requirements gatekeeper**: after confirmation it creates `requirements-contract.md`, assigning stable `REQ-<AREA>-NNN` IDs only to explicit owner requirements. The article lane G issues the immutable article contract and, after R approval, verifies only contract identity, hashes and whether applicable requirements remain traceable to delivery. W maintains `requirements-traceability.md` per article, mapping each applicable REQ ID to canonical and final-payload evidence. R owns complete article quality—language, facts, research, SEO, title strategy, localization, structure, images and payload fidelity. Missing, substituted, weakened or out-of-scope requirements are `REQUIREMENT-<AREA>-NNN`; article quality findings remain R's `SEO-*`, `CONTENT-*` and similar stable IDs. G must not repeat R's semantic SEO, source, image or payload analysis. Campaign G fills currently available runtime capacity with ready isolated work, then provisions the next priority-ready article worktree whenever a slot becomes available. Keep one visible, campaign-scoped `CAMPAIGN_OPERATIONS_STEWARD` in `state.json.orchestration.service_agents.campaign_operations` and resume it for readiness evidence, session recovery and bounded technical investigation. Do not create phase-named one-shot registration, preflight or recovery agents. No public-QA role is fresh: the existing article lane G performs its article's post-publication read-only comparison. Each role turn has a record in `state.json.orchestration.tasks[]` with an `agent_id`, role, bounded input paths, expected output path, timestamps, status and result reference. Each nonempty `article_workspaces[article_id]` record must state `root_role: ARTICLE_LANE_GATEKEEPER` and `role_bundle{lane_gatekeeper_agent,writer_agent,reviewer_agent}`. Track `available_slots`, `active_article_pairs`, `active_article_worktrees`, `spawn_policy` and `last_dispatch_at` in `state.json.orchestration.capacity`. A saved report without that task record is not an independent-role artifact.

For each `article_id`, create exactly one visible `ARTICLE_LANE_GATEKEEPER`, `ARTICLE_WRITER` and `ARTICLE_LANGUAGE_REVIEWER`; reuse those identities only for that article's drafting, repair, full R, R-Δ and article-level gate. This preserves article language, brand and finding continuity without allowing cross-article context bleed. The reviewer remains independent of its article W and lane G; an ordinary bounded review uses the article contract, review index, cited baseline and changed evidence, while a full reread is required only under the defined rehydration/escalation triggers. If replacement is unavoidable, record `ARTICLE_AGENT_REPLACED`, article ID, old/new IDs and reason, then require `ARTICLE_AGENT_CONTEXT_REHYDRATED` before the replacement reports. Never leave duplicate active lane Gs, writers or reviewers for the same article; queue whole article lanes only after all currently available runtime slots have been filled. After the human return receipt names `HUMAN_ACCEPTED`, reuse only that article's registered lane G for the reader-page check. It records its existing agent ID, receipt/snapshot inputs and `gate/public-qa-report-N.md`; R is not resumed, W is not invoked, and no new public gate is created.

CLI is reserved for deterministic local mechanics such as file reads, JSON/schema checks, hashes, bundled scripts and version-control operations. Detached shell jobs, hidden CLI conversations and CLI-launched agents must not stand in for an independent role, editorial verdict, probe or experimental evidence. If the runtime cannot create a visible child agent, record `VISIBLE_DELEGATION_UNAVAILABLE`, preserve the resume point and block that stage rather than fabricate an independent result.

## Finding contract

Use `AREA-RULE-NNN`, for example `SEO-HEADING-001` or `CONTENT-SOURCE-002`. Preserve an ID across wording, line or recommendation changes. Each report declares `OPEN`, `RESOLVED` or `SUPERSEDED`; the review index preserves the same lineage without minting replacement IDs. An approved review and a PASS gate contain no open quality finding.

## State contract

`prewrite_planning → awaiting_owner_prewrite_confirmation → research → write → review → repair → gate → human_release_ready → human_publishing → human_accepted → public_readonly_validating → completed`

Allowed terminal alternatives are `deferred`, `blocked` and `capacity_blocked`. `human_release_ready` is an authorization for a human hand-off, never an instruction to publish automatically.

## Public reader-page contract

The public check is a narrow, read-only transport-and-contract comparison, not another editorial review. The human first saves `handoff/public-return-receipt.json`: public URL, exact `HUMAN_ACCEPTED` or `HUMAN_NEEDS_FIX` state, return time, known publication revision/timestamp or `UNVERIFIED`, optional rendered-page evidence paths, and any known limitation. Validate it with `harnessctl.py check-public-return-receipt`, then write a bounded `evidence/public-qa/public-snapshot.json`. The snapshot records only normalized public observations—fetch/result metadata, page title, public-text fingerprint, link/image counts and records, required CTA anchor/href/disclosure observations, and snapshot hash. It is a comparison aid, not a new editorial artifact or a publishing operation.

Only after `HUMAN_ACCEPTED` does the pre-existing `ARTICLE_LANE_GATEKEEPER` consume that receipt/snapshot and make the reader-page comparison against the most recently accepted canonical article and visual payload: page title, **rendered visual hierarchy** of sections/subsections, reader-visible body, declared links, image presence and observable Alt/caption, plus the required CTA's exact visible anchor text, href and applicable disclosure. It writes `gate/public-qa-report-N.md` and one per-article `state.json.publication.articles[article_id]` record. That record binds the receipt's timezone-aware return time, local receipt/snapshot/report paths, registered lane-G ID, attempt and bounded `unverified_retry_count`; the files must exist and bind the same article/URL. Its visible task row is `workflow_stage: PUBLIC_QA_READONLY` with the same G ID, a timestamp and the report path. It does not start R-P, invoke W, create a fresh public gate, or redo SEO/prose review.

| G result | Next action |
| --- | --- |
| `PUBLIC_QA_PASSED` | Mark that article complete. |
| `PUBLIC_QA_PASSED_WITH_LIMITATION` | Mark that article complete only when every reader-visible contract item still passes and the limitation is fully scoped. |
| `HUMAN_TRANSPORT_FIX_REQUIRED` | Write one consolidated human platform-side repair checklist; after repair, reuse this same lane G for one new receipt/snapshot check. Do not invoke W/R. |
| `PUBLIC_QA_UNVERIFIED` | Preserve URL/evidence and allow at most the policy's one bounded same-lane G recapture/recheck. Do not infer a pass or invoke W/R. |
| `CANONICAL_CHANGE_REQUESTED` | Requires an explicit owner request ID naming a canonical/payload change. Only then re-enter W → R → G. |

On a mismatch, the same G opens a stable `PUBLIC-CONTRACT-NNN` finding (or `PUBLIC-VISUAL-HIERARCHY-NNN` for visual hierarchy). A known or accepted platform limitation must name platform, account/site, editor/theme, locale/market, observation date, evidence paths and affected contract items, set `not_generalizable: true`, and state its owner-acceptance status. It can never become a universal claim about a platform. A limitation that fails a reader-visible contract item is `HUMAN_TRANSPORT_FIX_REQUIRED` or owner decision territory, not a passing limitation.

## Title transfer and visual hierarchy contract

Default `SEPARATE_TITLE_FIELD` tells the human to copy the title and body separately. `TITLE_IN_BODY` is for a target without a distinct title field. A hand-off manifest states this local selection mode, but it is not evidence of an editor or public-page HTML structure; the legacy `BODY_H1_REQUIRED` value is input-compatible only and normalizes to `TITLE_IN_BODY`.

The canonical outline and visual-payload tags are authoring aids. **Only the rendered public reader page decides heading hierarchy.** After `HUMAN_ACCEPTED`, lane G uses visual reader-page evidence to check that the title, section headings and subsection headings are visibly distinct, ordered and not visibly duplicated or flattened. The normalized public snapshot may record whether visual evidence exists, but it cannot decide hierarchy. Editor HTML/DOM cannot be inspected, edited, requested from the human, or used as evidence. Public source/feed markup also cannot decide this hierarchy verdict. If rendered evidence is unavailable or inconclusive, write `PUBLIC_QA_UNVERIFIED`; do not infer success from the local payload.

## Cross-language SEO contract

When a campaign freezes a cross-language SEO target, W must record a dated global Google Trends discovery set and dated target-region SERP semantic evidence in `research/cross-language-seo.md`. For every target language, Google Trends compares **English seeds and English long-tail candidates only**; record `GT seed language = ENGLISH_ONLY`, the English source concept/query and its target-region SERP wording map. Select a relevant low-base/high-momentum concept from that global English set, then write for the regional intent with observed natural variants. Google Trends is relative-interest evidence, not local search-volume proof; regional SERP is localization evidence, not a license to copy competitors. If at least two independent target-region SERP checks show no usable consensus variant, W may record `MODEL_TRANSLATION_FALLBACK` with the failed evidence, proposed local wording, rationale and risk. R verifies fluency, intent and risk; G only verifies that this explicitly frozen localization task has mapped evidence and never calls a fallback SERP-proven. Otherwise it requires the map or an explicit owner waiver before accepting the task contract.

When a current public brand-site variant serves the target locale and article intent, it is the highest-priority wording source: `CURRENT_BRAND_SITE → REGIONAL_SERP → MODEL_TRANSLATION_FALLBACK`. Record its URL, context and date; only a documented stale, misleading, unnatural or intent-conflicting condition permits a lower-priority choice. This maximizes message consistency and the brand-effect matrix.

## Scope contract

Only pairs in `campaign.json.platform_scope.allowed_pairs` may appear in a platform release package or state record. Empty scope is valid for non-publishing work. No Skill can discover or append a platform/account pair.
