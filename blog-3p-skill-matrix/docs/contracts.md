# Shared contracts

## Canonical record

Every article has one `article_id` and one canonical source. The package manifest records `canonical_sha256`; a reader-visible change to the canonical source or visual payload creates a new fingerprint and re-enters W → R → G. A public-page observation alone follows the public reader-page contract below and does not reopen W/R. `context/article-contract.json` and `reviews/review-index.json` are compact, hash-bound navigation records; neither can alter canonical content, owner scope or an approved verdict.

## Role contract

| Role | May do | Must not do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| W | Draft and repair canonical content | Review its own work or decide release |
| R | Independently inspect and issue findings | Edit the article or change scope |
| G | Collect and report the pre-write plan, freeze the owner contract after confirmation, dispatch, decide contract acceptance and update state; resolve a recorded topic-evidence exception only inside its frozen slot | Draft text, claim W research is complete, re-review article quality, silently change a frozen topic or publish |
| Human | Use a native platform UI, accept or flag the reader page, and save the URL/state return receipt | Change canonical content without a correction record |

## Orchestration contract

### Pre-write owner-confirmation contract

Before any article artifact path, W, R, article queue, outline, prose, image, canonical package, or article-research subagent exists, campaign G maintains canonical `prewrite-plan.json` and runs `harnessctl.py sync-prewrite-plan` to render the owner-facing `prewrite-plan.md`. In current schema-2.13 workspaces the manifest is schema `1.7`; the Markdown report is a deterministic read-only view, never a separately hand-maintained plan. It has one shared `campaign_strategy` plus one card for every configured `article_id`: `task_and_audience`, `reader_value_and_secondary_cta`, `editorial_brief`, and `risks_and_owner_decisions`. Each card also shows a `frozen_delivery_mapping`, `topic_slot`, `evidence_posture`, and `review_effort`. `topic_slot` freezes the reader task, core intent, market, differentiation angle and prohibited deviations, but not literal keywords or the final title. The editorial brief consolidates the proposed reader problem, keyword/localization route, fact/source boundary, title/outline, visual narrative and known transport assumptions so the owner sees a complete research-and-writing plan without paying to maintain nine duplicate planning fields. It may cite an actual campaign shared-evidence pack, but never creates an empty cache merely to satisfy the plan. Schema-2.12 and older plans remain historical read/check compatibility only.

The owner must explicitly return `OWNER_PREWRITE_PLAN_CONFIRMED`. In schema 2.13, G first preserves the owner-originated receipt under `evidence/owner-confirmations/`, then uses `confirm-prewrite-plan` to bind the exact article IDs, confirmation ID, report SHA-256, manifest SHA-256, protected scope snapshot/hash, receipt path/hash, locator and timezone-aware timestamp in state, confirmation, requirements contract, and scope lock. A manually edited status or ID alone cannot promote the plan. The protected snapshot includes each article's language, market, platform, account, audience-fit mode, cross-language exception, `topic_slot`, and evidence posture. If any of these changes, `sync-prewrite-plan` returns `SCOPE_RECONFIRM_REQUIRED` without overwriting the old receipt; an explicit invalidation preserves the old record and returns the plan to owner confirmation. Before W/R dispatch, every article also needs the owner-confirmed one-to-one `{platform, account}` assignment and compatible audience/transport row. `OWNER_PREWRITE_PLAN_CHANGES_REQUESTED` never permits a partial dispatch. The dossier is a plan and known-evidence record, not `RESEARCH_READY` or `RESEARCH_APPROVED`: after confirmation W still conducts integrated research. In the default route, independent R covers that research inside its final full review; only an elevated route separately approves research before drafting.

### Article contract, review index, and delta capsule

At dispatch, the campaign G/harness writes one immutable `context/article-contract.json` for each article. It projects only the confirmed article-relevant scope: article ID, task/language/market, applicable `REQ-*`, reader-value/required CTA declaration, release-pair and locale references when applicable, plus paths and hashes for its confirmed campaign records. It deliberately excludes mutable open findings. It is derived from the global scope lock and cannot add, remove or reinterpret a requirement; this mechanical projection is not an extra article-G turn.

R maintains `reviews/review-index.json` after every required full review, any elevated research review, and R-Δ. It indexes the frozen `review_effort`, latest decisions, current canonical/package hashes, report paths, mutable open/resolved/superseded stable finding IDs and the next required input. In `STANDARD_INTEGRATED_REVIEW`, `latest_research_review` is explicitly `NOT_REQUIRED / INTEGRATED_IN_FULL_REVIEW`; in `ELEVATED_EARLY_CHALLENGE`, a separate approved research record is required. Ordinary W/R/G turns load the contract, index and current changed artifacts; they do not reread every historic report merely because another bounded turn begins. After any context compression, first reread [工作流核心](workflow-core.md), then these compact records. A full historical reread is mandatory only for referenced hash drift, unresolved or ambiguous finding lineage, agent replacement, scope conflict or explicit escalation. The compact records point to evidence; they never overrule it.

An R-Δ begins with `reviews/review-delta-N.json`. It names the approved baseline hashes/report, exact changed paths, affected claims, `REQ-*` and stable finding IDs. R reads that capsule, changed artifacts and direct dependencies; it does not reload unrelated history because one finding exists. Missing or contradictory data, hash drift, ambiguous finding lineage, a changed reader-value promise, or a scope change escalates to full R. After a final full review, `R_VISUAL_DELTA` remains limited to visual assets, the visual manifest, payload or the package's visual pointer; it must bind the current canonical, metadata, visual-manifest, payload and package hashes.

### Current artifact-source contract

For a current schema-2.13 workspace, each `article-package.json@1.5` lives in `articles/<article_id>/` and is a one-way source index: `canonical/article.html`, `canonical/metadata.json`, `research/evidence-pack.json` and `canonical/visual-manifest.json`, plus frozen reader-value/CTA declarations. The compiler must consume exactly those package-declared paths and hashes; it cannot accept a second body, title, metadata or visual source. The evidence pack schema `1.3` declares `topic_slot_alignment`, `reader_intent_basis`, optional bounded `platform_profile_use`, `evidence_posture`, and—only when real reuse occurs—`campaign_shared_evidence.path`, `.sha256`, `.record_ids`, plus `article_delta.decision`, `.freshness_or_scope_check`, and `.additional_evidence_refs`. Those structure fields prevent scope/evidence drift but do not score prose. `ALIGNED` can list in-slot wording adjustments. `TOPIC_EVIDENCE_CONFLICT` blocks full review until one registered G task records `KEEP_SLOT` or `NARROW_WITHIN_SLOT`; the resolved record retains its reason and evidence references. A different reader task, market or promise still invalidates the pre-write confirmation. The package never points back to the derived handoff manifest and does not repeat SEO metadata, link lists or image lists. W compiles paired final visual payloads before `FULL_REVIEW`: `visual-payload.html` is the rich-text transfer and semantic-review primary; `visual-payload.md` is its mechanically derived readable counterpart. R semantically reads the HTML primary and the package-declared sources. The validator proves the companion projection when possible; only its explicit `COMPANION_DUAL_READ_REQUIRED` result requires R to semantically read Markdown too. In the default route, `reviews/review-index.json.latest_full_review` also binds the evidence-pack hash; it always binds the canonical, metadata, visual-manifest, HTML payload, Markdown payload and package hashes. Every asset has one ordered canonical-body marker (`<!-- BLOG_3P_IMAGE:01 -->` onward), a manifest `ordinal`, a visible placement anchor and an existing, hash-matched `01-lead-*` / `02-middle-*` / `03-closing-*` PNG or JPG/JPEG file; aggregate image slots and WebP/GIF/SVG handoff assets are invalid. If these files remain unchanged, no third review exists. Post-R handoff finalization may only emit a manifest from byte-identical renders of both reviewed payloads; it must fail before overwriting either changed candidate. W compiles any changed candidate before the appropriate same-R delta or full review. If only a post-review visual/payload change occurs, same-R `R_VISUAL_DELTA` binds the new hashes; unclear, lineage-breaking or scope-changing changes return to full R. The handoff manifest is derived once, points forward to the package and review index, and G checks only that chain plus `REQ-*` traceability. Schema `2.12 / 1.4` and older packages retain historical compatibility rules unless deliberately migrated.

The campaign shared-evidence pack is immutable within one confirmed content project and only contains real, bounded, reusable observations. A receiving article may cite it only with the exact key, current hash and record IDs; W still records the article's local decision and any required freshness check, while R reads only those cited records and the article delta rather than the whole shared pack. Shared evidence never supplies volatile claims, account state, platform selection, or `MODEL_TRANSLATION_FALLBACK`. A changed shared-pack hash does not silently amend an approved article: only articles that cite the changed record re-enter the applicable delta or full review.

### Mechanical preflight and observation

Immediately before `FULL_REVIEW`, W runs `harnessctl.py check-review-ready`. It may reject only stale contract/index bindings, absent or hash-drifted artifacts, malformed fixed payload structure, frozen CTA transport drift, or an unverified compiler companion; it does not issue an editorial, factual, or SEO verdict. When no `COMPANION_DUAL_READ_REQUIRED` fallback is emitted, HTML is the normal R semantic surface; that explicit fallback requires both projected payloads to be read. A failure returns to W to repair the mechanical package. `harnessctl.py summarize-efficiency` is optional and reads only the completed task ledger. It records a metric as `UNAVAILABLE` when runtime token, duration, or tool-call data was not supplied; it does not estimate cost, create a role, or gate release.

## Article-platform assignment contract

Before editorial dispatch for every current human-native release, freeze `article_platform_assignment.assignments[]`. It is a one-to-one mapping from every `article_id` to one owner-supplied `{platform, account}` in `platform_scope.allowed_pairs`. Article IDs, platform names and pairs must each be unique; a verified Blogger account, for example, is not a generic allocation pool. Missing/duplicate assignment is `ARTICLE_PLATFORM_ASSIGNMENT_BLOCKED_RECONFIRM_OWNER`. G may continue other fully mapped articles, but must not borrow, rotate, substitute or discover a platform; a remap needs fresh owner confirmation.

## Locale-platform compatibility contract

Freeze one `locale_platform_validation.rows[]` record per current article before W/R. A record contains its article ID, user-confirmed article language and market, mapped platform/account, `primary_reader_languages`, `primary_reader_markets`, `primary_audience_evidence_path`, `transport_supported_content_languages`, `transport_evidence_path`, and a `fit_mode`. The article language directs research and writing. Transport support only decides whether the format can carry the language; it never proves the primary reader audience. The ordinary decision is `PRIMARY_AUDIENCE_MATCH`. If language or market needs an exception, require `CROSS_LANGUAGE_EXCEPTION_OWNER_CONFIRMED` with the same exact owner wording/confirmation ID as the selection receipt; otherwise report `PLATFORM_AUDIENCE_MISMATCH_RECONFIRM_OWNER`. Do not translate the article or automatically reassign its platform.

## Title quality and field-mapping contract

Freeze focus keyword/approved variants, reader task, canonical article title, explicit platform title and SEO title per article. W/R judge semantic quality from topic clarity, reader intent, distinct value, natural language and market fit; G only verifies that an owner-specified title or SEO task survives into delivery. Literal keyword coverage and language-appropriate length are advisory signals, never standalone failure tests. The default mapping is `platform_title = canonical_title`; a platform title field must never receive a shorter substitute while a stronger title exists elsewhere. A clearly keyword-only, generic, misleading or task-mismatched title is `SEO-TITLE-INTENT-NNN`; an unapproved mapping divergence is `TITLE-FIELD-MAP-NNN`. Repair all linked title fields, fingerprint and release card together, then re-review.

## Content-first recommendation contract

The primary outcome of every article is an evidence-backed, standalone answer to its frozen reader task. A campaign CTA is required in every new schema-2.2 article, but never a completion quota or substitute for instruction, analysis, examples, evidence, or a balanced product comparison. It remains a secondary, contextually relevant recommendation. An explicitly requested product review or comparison may have a product-specific reader task, but it must still answer that task independently rather than becoming sales copy.

For the required secondary CTA, freeze a non-empty `reader_value_promise` and `cta` declaration in the article record: exact visible `anchor_text`, product identity, current destination, product/claim evidence path, reader-task relevance, and exact relationship disclosure or `NOT_APPLICABLE`. A current schema-2.13 `article-package.json@1.5` references its package-declared canonical artifact sources and copies those declarations exactly; `harnessctl.py check-article-package` verifies this mapping and the artifact chain without judging the prose, and the hand-off compiler/validator verify the visible anchor, href and applicable disclosure in the body. Existing schema-2.12/1.4 and older packages remain historical compatibility only. W must trace product claims to the evidence pack and must not invent reliability, rankings, tests, availability, outcomes, or independent endorsement. R decides whether the article remains useful without the CTA and whether the recommendation is genuinely secondary, relevant, evidence-bounded, and transparent. G only checks that the owner-confirmed declaration and required CTA/disclosure survive into the canonical package and visual hand-off; it does not judge product quality or rewrite commercial language.

This policy reduces obvious promotional-spam and disclosure risk; it does not promise that a platform will retain a public page or replace current platform policies.

### Current schema-2.13 main-session execution

The default is `CONTINUOUS_CAMPAIGN_MAIN_SESSION` with `MAIN_SESSION_PATH_ISOLATED`. Each article has one disjoint root, `articles/<article_id>/`, containing `context`, `research`, `canonical`, `reviews` and `handoff`; the article contract, evidence pack, canonical source, review index and handoff files stay within that root. Visible W/R role sessions may be reused across ordinary articles, but each turn is bound to one article's paths and records. R remains independent of W and issues a separate review for every article; shared G remains the campaign control plane.

`GIT_WORKTREE` is an explicit exception only. Its `worktree_dispatch_decision` must record exactly one of `TRUE_CONCURRENT_WRITE`, `HIGH_RISK_REWRITE_OR_ROLLBACK` or `OWNER_REQUESTED_GIT_ISOLATION`. Token saving, an available slot or general capacity is never a reason to create a worktree; scheduling capacity only decides which safe, ready article proceeds next.

### Historical worktree-first behavior (schema-2.12 and earlier)

The owner pre-authorizes visible child-task delegation by default **after the pre-write confirmation passes**. Visibility, reusable role identity and Git worktree isolation are separate properties: a child task is not automatically a worktree. G records host capability before dispatch. When the project can create worktrees, campaign G creates one visible project-worktree task per ready independent article before the first W turn, waits for a usable worktree/task identifier, and records `GIT_WORKTREE`, task ID, path when exposed, base ref and lifecycle in `state.json.orchestration.article_workspaces[article_id]`. The worktree root is the article's reusable W/R pair, never a per-article G task. Its `role_bundle` records that W/R pair and the same registered `campaign_gatekeeper_agent` used by every article only for traceability. When worktrees are unavailable, campaign G records `WORKTREE_UNAVAILABLE` with the reason; a visible shared-workspace fallback is permitted only when article paths are disjoint, must be recorded as `VISIBLE_SHARED_WORKSPACE_WITH_PATH_ISOLATION`, and must never be represented as a worktree. Overlapping paths are `WORKTREE_ISOLATION_BLOCKED` and remain queued.

Campaign G is the visible controller and persistent **global owner-requirements gatekeeper**: after confirmation it creates `requirements-contract.md`, assigning stable `REQ-<AREA>-NNN` IDs only to explicit owner requirements. W maintains `requirements-traceability.md` per article, mapping each applicable REQ ID to canonical and final-payload evidence. R owns complete article quality—language, facts, research, SEO, title strategy, localization, structure, images and payload fidelity. Missing, substituted, weakened or out-of-scope requirements are `REQUIREMENT-<AREA>-NNN`; article quality findings remain R's `SEO-*`, `CONTENT-*` and similar stable IDs. G must not repeat R's semantic SEO, source, image or payload analysis. Instead, it runs one `BATCH_GATE_ACCEPTANCE` task for the exact set of currently R-approved rows, verifying frozen declarations, artifact hashes, reviewer approval and REQ traceability. A row can be `HUMAN_RELEASE_READY` while another is `CHANGES_REQUIRED`; a not-yet-ready article never holds the batch. Each role turn has a record in `state.json.orchestration.tasks[]` with an `agent_id`, bounded input paths, expected and actual output paths, timestamps, status and result reference. The batch task also records its exact `article_ids`; its report has one independently hash-bound row per article. Campaign G fills currently available runtime capacity with ready isolated work, then provisions the next priority-ready article worktree whenever a slot becomes available. Keep one visible, campaign-scoped `CAMPAIGN_OPERATIONS_STEWARD` in `state.json.orchestration.service_agents.campaign_operations` and resume it for readiness evidence, session recovery and bounded technical investigation. Do not create phase-named one-shot registration, preflight or recovery agents. Track `available_slots`, `active_article_pairs`, `active_article_worktrees`, `spawn_policy` and `last_dispatch_at` in `state.json.orchestration.capacity`. A saved report without that task record is not an independent-role artifact.

For each `article_id`, create exactly one visible `ARTICLE_WRITER` and `ARTICLE_LANGUAGE_REVIEWER`; reuse those identities only for that article's drafting, repair, full R and R-Δ. This preserves article language, brand and finding continuity without allowing cross-article context bleed. The reviewer remains independent of its article W; an ordinary bounded review uses the article contract, review index, cited baseline and changed evidence, while a full reread is required only under the defined rehydration/escalation triggers. If replacement is unavoidable, record `ARTICLE_AGENT_REPLACED`, article ID, old/new IDs and reason, then require `ARTICLE_AGENT_CONTEXT_REHYDRATED` before the replacement reports. Never leave duplicate active writers or reviewers for the same article; queue whole article W/R pairs only after all currently available runtime slots have been filled. After a human return receipt names `HUMAN_ACCEPTED`, the registered campaign G may include that article in its next `PUBLIC_QA_BATCH_READONLY` task. The batch report records a separate receipt/snapshot/visual-evidence-bound result per article; R is not resumed, W is not invoked, and no fresh public gate is created.

CLI is reserved for deterministic local mechanics such as file reads, JSON/schema checks, hashes, bundled scripts and version-control operations. Detached shell jobs, hidden CLI conversations and CLI-launched agents must not stand in for an independent role, editorial verdict, probe or experimental evidence. If the runtime cannot create a visible child agent, record `VISIBLE_DELEGATION_UNAVAILABLE`, preserve the resume point and block that stage rather than fabricate an independent result.

## Finding contract

Use `AREA-RULE-NNN`, for example `SEO-HEADING-001` or `CONTENT-SOURCE-002`. Preserve an ID across wording, line or recommendation changes. Each report declares `OPEN`, `RESOLVED` or `SUPERSEDED`; the review index preserves the same lineage without minting replacement IDs. An approved review and a PASS gate contain no open quality finding.

## State contract

`prewrite_planning → awaiting_owner_prewrite_confirmation → research → write → review → repair → gate → human_release_ready → human_publishing → human_accepted → public_readonly_validating → completed`

Allowed terminal alternatives are `deferred`, `blocked` and `capacity_blocked`. `human_release_ready` is an authorization for a human hand-off, never an instruction to publish automatically.

For `STANDARD_INTEGRATED_REVIEW`, the coarse `research → write` state transition is W's internal continuous sequence, not an R approval boundary; R first enters at `review`. For `ELEVATED_EARLY_CHALLENGE`, the separate research-review record is the additional boundary before drafting.

## Public reader-page contract

The public check is a narrow, read-only transport-and-contract comparison, not another editorial review. The human first saves `handoff/public-return-receipt.json`: public URL, exact `HUMAN_ACCEPTED` or `HUMAN_NEEDS_FIX` state, return time, known publication revision/timestamp or `UNVERIFIED`, optional rendered-page evidence paths, and any known limitation. Validate it with `harnessctl.py check-public-return-receipt`, then write a bounded `evidence/public-qa/public-snapshot.json`. The snapshot records only normalized public observations—fetch/result metadata, page title, public-text fingerprint, link/image counts and records, required CTA anchor/href/disclosure observations, and snapshot hash. It is a comparison aid, not a new editorial artifact or a publishing operation.

Only after `HUMAN_ACCEPTED` does the registered `CAMPAIGN_GATEKEEPER` consume the currently returned receipt/snapshot set in one `PUBLIC_QA_BATCH_READONLY` task and make the reader-page comparison against each most recently accepted canonical article and visual payload: page title, **rendered visual hierarchy** of sections/subsections, reader-visible body, declared links, image presence and observable Alt/caption, plus the required CTA's exact visible anchor text, href and applicable disclosure. It writes one batch report and one per-article `state.json.publication.articles[article_id]` record. Every record binds its own receipt's timezone-aware return time, local receipt/snapshot paths, registered campaign-G ID, batch/entry ID and hash, attempt and bounded `unverified_retry_count`; the files must exist and bind the same article/URL. The visible task row is `workflow_stage: PUBLIC_QA_BATCH_READONLY` with the same G ID, an exact article-ID set, timestamp and report path. It does not start R-P, invoke W, create a fresh public gate, or redo SEO/prose review.

| G result | Next action |
| --- | --- |
| `PUBLIC_QA_PASSED` | Mark that article complete. |
| `PUBLIC_QA_PASSED_WITH_LIMITATION` | Mark that article complete only when every reader-visible contract item still passes and the limitation is fully scoped. |
| `HUMAN_TRANSPORT_FIX_REQUIRED` | Write one consolidated human platform-side repair checklist; after repair, include only the affected article in the same G's next receipt/snapshot batch. Do not invoke W/R. |
| `PUBLIC_QA_UNVERIFIED` | Preserve URL/evidence and allow at most the policy's one bounded same-G recapture/recheck. Do not infer a pass or invoke W/R. |
| `CANONICAL_CHANGE_REQUESTED` | Requires an explicit owner request ID naming a canonical/payload change. Only then re-enter that article's W → R path. |

On a mismatch, the same G opens a stable `PUBLIC-CONTRACT-NNN` finding (or `PUBLIC-VISUAL-HIERARCHY-NNN` for visual hierarchy). A known or accepted platform limitation must name platform, account/site, editor/theme, locale/market, observation date, evidence paths and affected contract items, set `not_generalizable: true`, and state its owner-acceptance status. It can never become a universal claim about a platform. A limitation that fails a reader-visible contract item is `HUMAN_TRANSPORT_FIX_REQUIRED` or owner decision territory, not a passing limitation.

## Title transfer and visual hierarchy contract

Default `SEPARATE_TITLE_FIELD` tells the human to copy the title and body separately. `TITLE_IN_BODY` is for a target without a distinct title field. A hand-off manifest states this local selection mode, but it is not evidence of an editor or public-page HTML structure; the legacy `BODY_H1_REQUIRED` value is input-compatible only and normalizes to `TITLE_IN_BODY`.

The canonical outline and visual-payload tags are authoring aids. **Only the rendered public reader page decides heading hierarchy.** After `HUMAN_ACCEPTED`, campaign G uses visual reader-page evidence in the article's batch entry to check that the title, section headings and subsection headings are visibly distinct, ordered and not visibly duplicated or flattened. The normalized public snapshot may record whether visual evidence exists, but it cannot decide hierarchy. Editor HTML/DOM cannot be inspected, edited, requested from the human, or used as evidence. Public source/feed markup also cannot decide this hierarchy verdict. If rendered evidence is unavailable or inconclusive, write `PUBLIC_QA_UNVERIFIED`; do not infer success from the local payload.

## Cross-language SEO contract

When a campaign freezes a cross-language SEO target, W must record dated target-region SERP semantic evidence and, when it is usable, optional global Google Trends context in the single research fact source `research/evidence-pack.json`. `research/cross-language-seo.md` may be rendered from that source as a readable view, but must not become a second hand-maintained record. Google Trends compares **English seeds and English long-tail candidates only**; record `GT seed language = ENGLISH_ONLY`, the English source concept/query, scope, window and capture date. It is global relative-interest context, not a prerequisite for research or drafting: a relative-interest screen, `Breakout`, or rising label cannot prove search volume, low-base/high-momentum, local demand, commercial intent, or model capability. When Trends data is unavailable, insufficient, or inconclusive, W must not force repeated collection or manufacture a trend rationale. Instead, choose the long-tail reader problem and intent from current brand-site wording and target-region SERP semantics; record the evidence path, uncertainty boundary, and that no popularity or demand claim is being made. Platform material is separately recorded as `platform_profile_use` and may only inform technical depth, examples, tone or transfer risk; it cannot establish a keyword, natural variant, search intent, local demand, popularity or topic demand. Regional SERP is localization evidence, not a license to copy competitors. If at least two independent target-region SERP checks show no usable consensus variant, W may record `MODEL_TRANSLATION_FALLBACK` with the failed evidence, proposed local wording, rationale and risk. R verifies fluency, intent, evidence boundaries, the declared `evidence_posture`, and false claims in its existing full review; G only verifies that this explicitly frozen localization task has mapped evidence and must not block solely because Trends lacks a clear signal. `METHOD_TEMPLATE_NO_EXECUTION` cannot claim A/B results, validation, stability, performance or causality. `DOCUMENTED_EMPIRICAL_RECORD` requires a protocol, inputs/settings, run log, evaluation criteria and limitations. Neither role may call a fallback SERP-proven. Otherwise G requires the applicable map or an explicit owner waiver before accepting the task contract.

When a current public brand-site variant serves the target locale and article intent, it is the highest-priority wording source: `CURRENT_BRAND_SITE → REGIONAL_SERP → MODEL_TRANSLATION_FALLBACK`. Record its URL, context and date; only a documented stale, misleading, unnatural or intent-conflicting condition permits a lower-priority choice. This maximizes message consistency and the brand-effect matrix.

## Scope contract

Only pairs in `campaign.json.platform_scope.allowed_pairs` may appear in a platform release package or state record. In current schema 2.13, empty scope, incomplete assignments, missing audience/transport records, or an unconfirmed cross-language exception block W/R dispatch because every article uses the human-native release path. No Skill can discover, append, substitute, or reuse a platform/account pair. Schema 2.12 and older data retain only historical read/check compatibility and cannot bypass this current dispatch rule.
